GPX to STL & 3MF Converter

Turn a GPX hiking, running, or cycling track into a real, 3D-printable terrain model — not just the flat line of your route, but the actual mountains, valleys, and elevation it crosses.

Quick answer: TrailPrint3D is a free Blender add-on that converts a GPX file into a 3D terrain mesh built from real elevation data, then exports it as an STL or 3MF file ready for any slicer. Download it free — no account, no upload, no watermark.

STL vs. 3MF — which one should you export?

STL is the simplest, most universal 3D printing format: a single mesh with no color or material info. It works in every slicer ever made, which makes it the safest choice if you just want one solid-color terrain print.

3MF is the modern alternative — it can hold several objects, colors, and materials in a single file. If your map has separate color regions (water, forest, buildings) or a multi-color trail, 3MF lets your slicer (Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer/OrcaSlicer) keep those parts separate automatically instead of you re-assembling loose STL files by hand.

TrailPrint3D exports both: STL and OBJ natively, and 3MF through the free 3mf Blender add-on by Clonephaze (one extra free install, a couple of clicks).

How to convert a GPX file to STL or 3MF

  1. Install Blender. Blender is free and open-source — get it from blender.org (version 5.1 or higher).
  2. Install the TrailPrint3D add-on. Grab it free from the download page, or from MakerWorld / Printables.
  3. Load your GPX file. Open the add-on's sidebar in Blender and select your GPX track — exported from Strava, Komoot, Garmin, or any GPS app.
  4. Generate the terrain. TrailPrint3D fetches real elevation data for the area and builds a 3D terrain mesh following your actual route, with options for shape, scale, and coloring.
  5. Export. Export directly as STL or OBJ, or use the Clonephaze add-on to export a multi-color 3MF ready for your slicer.

Why not just use a simple online GPX converter?

Plenty of web tools will trace a GPX line into a flat, extruded shape. TrailPrint3D does something different: it builds the surrounding terrain from real elevation data, so the print shows the actual ridges, valleys, and summits your route crosses — not just a raised outline on a flat base. On top of that you get real building and road data from OpenStreetMap, automatic landscape coloring, and a choice of shapes (square, circle, hexagon, jigsaw puzzle, and more) — all inside Blender, where you keep full control before exporting.

FAQ

Is there a free way to convert GPX to STL?

Yes. TrailPrint3D's core add-on is completely free and exports STL (and OBJ) directly — no account, no file uploads, no watermark. It runs locally inside Blender.

Can I convert a GPX file directly to 3MF?

Yes, via the free 3mf Blender add-on by Clonephaze, which TrailPrint3D integrates with for multi-color/multi-object 3MF export.

Do I need Blender experience to do this?

No. TrailPrint3D handles the terrain generation and export for you through its own sidebar panel — you don't need prior Blender modeling skills, just the free software installed.

Will the STL include real elevation, or just a flat extruded line?

Real elevation. TrailPrint3D downloads actual terrain height data for the area around your GPX track and builds a true 3D terrain mesh, not a flat shape with the route stamped on top.
